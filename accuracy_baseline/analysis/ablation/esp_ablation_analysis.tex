Table~\ref{tab:esp_ablation_svhn} isolates the contribution of each ESP component on SVHN.
Local sparsity alone (LS=0.5) reduces the upload volume by 8.5\% (from 26699.8~MB to 24420.5~MB, i.e., 1.09$\times$ compression) but degrades accuracy by 1.86 percentage points (from 95.13\% to 93.27\%), because independent client-side selections are not aligned and can discard useful updates.
Adding residual feedback (RF) recovers 1.44 percentage points of accuracy (to 94.71\%) at the cost of a slight upload increase, since residuals preserve information from the dropped packs. Introducing per-round decay (PRD) and cross-round decay (CRD) further stabilizes convergence, raising accuracy to 94.91\%.
Finally, rank voting (RK=0.7) aligns sparse selections across clients and avoids ciphertext union expansion; this cuts the upload volume by 23.3\% relative to LS+RF+PRD+CRD and by 29.9\% (1.4$\times$) relative to the baseline, down to 18729.7~MB, while improving accuracy to 95.05\%.
Thus, every ESP component is necessary: RF prevents information loss from sparsification, decay factors stabilize residual aggregation, and RK delivers the upstream savings that make ECP practical.

Table~\ref{tab:communication} reports the per-client communication breakdown for CIFAR-10 with union voting and prefetch window $R=3$.
Without ECP, every selected client would have to synchronously download the full downstream payload, creating severe straggler bottlenecks on slow clients.
ECP shifts the vast majority of this traffic into asynchronous prefetching: the pre-downloaded volume (PV) accounts for 97.0--100\% of each client's total downstream volume, while the synchronous fetch volume (DV) is reduced to 0--14.59~MB.
Notably, Clients 1, 3, and 5 achieve DV = 0.00~MB, meaning their models are fully synchronized through background accumulation alone.
Even for active clients such as Client 0 and Client 4, which participate in 34 and 38 rounds and upload 2207 and 2472 ciphertext batches, the synchronous portion is only 2.0\% of the total downstream volume.
This confirms that ECP transforms the downstream bottleneck into latency-tolerant background traffic, making the training wall-clock time insensitive to individual downlink bandwidth.
Moreover, ECP is architecturally coupled with ESP: the sparse ciphertext batches produced by ESP are exactly the units that ECP prefetches and merges locally; without ESP, each prefetch would carry a full model and the bandwidth saving would disappear, while without ECP the sparse updates would still require a synchronous download round that reintroduces stragglers.
