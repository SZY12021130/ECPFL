\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
Cross-device federated learning suffers from severe bidirectional communication overhead when homomorphically encrypted model updates are exchanged over bandwidth-constrained edge networks.
To address this challenge, we propose ECPFL, a secure CKKS-based framework that co-designs efficient sparse packing with ciphertext prefetching.
ECPFL harmonizes pack-level sparsification with CKKS SIMD slot alignment through distributed voting and shifts the dominant downstream traffic into asynchronous client idle periods via residual ciphertext prefetching.
Comprehensive evaluations across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and SVHN show that ECPFL preserves baseline model accuracy while reducing downstream communication overhead by 35 to 292 times and attaining end-to-end training speedups of up to 4.5 times in communication-dominated regimes.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
This work makes three contributions.
First, we propose ECPFL, the first secure cross-device FL framework that combines ciphertext prefetching with encrypted sparse packing to simultaneously reduce upstream and downstream communication.
Second, we design a distributed voting-based sparse packing protocol that guarantees SIMD slot alignment across heterogeneous clients without trusted hardware.
Third, we conduct extensive experiments demonstrating that ECPFL reduces per-round downstream communication by 97.2\% to 99.7\% and achieves end-to-end training speedups of up to 4.5 times compared with state-of-the-art secure FL baselines.

\subsection{Overhead and Efficiency Evaluation}
\label{sec:overhead-efficiency}

\subsubsection{Communication overhead}
Table~\ref{tab:perf_evaluation} reports the per-round upload batches, aggregated batches, communication volumes, and end-to-end latency, while Figure~\ref{communication_bar} visualizes the total upstream and downstream traffic accumulated over the 50 training rounds. Both demonstrate that ECPFL-RK substantially alleviates the downstream communication bottleneck while maintaining upstream traffic comparable to the most efficient baselines.

Specifically, ECPFL-RK reduces the synchronous downstream volume from 87.7~MB, 79.7~MB, 534.0~MB, and 650.7~MB to 0.3~MB, 0.4~MB, 7.7~MB, and 11.1~MB per round on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and SVHN, respectively. Consequently, the downstream reductions relative to FedAvg+CKKS are 292, 199, 69, and 59 fold. These gains originate from the pack-level sparse consensus, which confines aggregation to 2, 3, 47, and 80 ciphertext batches per round, together with the ECP module that shifts the dominant server-to-client payload into asynchronous prefetching.

Figure~\ref{communication_bar} further confirms the aggregate trend: ECPFL-TH consistently records the smallest downstream traffic on all four datasets, and its upstream volume is comparable to or smaller than that of FedPHE and ESAFL. Therefore, ciphertext prefetching effectively removes the synchronous downstream bottleneck without inflating total upstream communication.

\subsubsection{End-to-End Training Time}
The UT, DT, and TT columns in Table~\ref{tab:perf_evaluation} are 50-round totals measured at the slowest client, where UT and DT denote upload and download wall-clock time, and TT is the sum of per-round slowest-client wall-clock time including local training, encryption, aggregation, and communication.

ECPFL-RK completes the 50 rounds on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and SVHN in 129.9~s, 95.8~s, 1441.4~s, and 923.2~s, respectively, whereas FedAvg+CKKS requires 315.0~s, 285.4~s, 1756.3~s, and 3141.6~s. Thus, ECPFL-RK achieves speedups of 2.4, 3.0, 1.2, and 3.4 times over FedAvg+CKKS. Furthermore, on Fashion-MNIST, ECPFL-RK outperforms FedPHE, ESAFL, and MASER by 1.9, 2.5, and 4.5 times, respectively.

On CIFAR-10, however, ECPFL-RK is marginally slower than FedPHE and ESAFL. This is because ECPFL-RK uploads 1175 ciphertext batches per round and aggregates 47 batches, whereas FedPHE uploads only 850 batches and aggregates 34 batches. As a result, ECPFL-RK incurs higher upload time (1355.7~s versus 831.6~s) and encryption overhead, which outweighs its much smaller download time (35.5~s versus 789.6~s). Nonetheless, ECPFL-RK still outperforms FedAvg+CKKS and MASER on CIFAR-10, and its synchronous downstream waiting time remains the lowest among all baselines on every dataset.
