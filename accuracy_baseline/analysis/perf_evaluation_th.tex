\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
Cross-device federated learning suffers from severe bidirectional communication overhead when homomorphically encrypted model updates are exchanged over bandwidth-constrained edge networks.
To address this challenge, we propose ECPFL, a secure CKKS-based framework that co-designs efficient sparse packing with ciphertext prefetching.
ECPFL harmonizes pack-level sparsification with CKKS SIMD slot alignment through distributed voting and shifts the dominant downstream traffic into asynchronous client idle periods via residual ciphertext prefetching.
Comprehensive evaluations across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and SVHN show that ECPFL preserves baseline model accuracy while reducing downstream communication overhead by 32 to 140 times and attaining end-to-end training speedups of up to 5.7 times in communication-dominated regimes.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
This work makes three contributions.
First, we propose ECPFL, the first secure cross-device FL framework that combines ciphertext prefetching with encrypted sparse packing to simultaneously reduce upstream and downstream communication.
Second, we design a distributed voting-based sparse packing protocol that guarantees SIMD slot alignment across heterogeneous clients without trusted hardware.
Third, we conduct extensive experiments demonstrating that ECPFL reduces per-round downstream communication by 97.0\% to 99.3\% and achieves end-to-end training speedups of up to 5.7 times compared with state-of-the-art secure FL baselines.

\subsection{Overhead and Efficiency Evaluation}
\label{sec:overhead-efficiency}

\subsubsection{Communication overhead}
Table~\ref{tab:perf_evaluation} and Figure~\ref{communication_bar} jointly demonstrate that ECPFL markedly alleviates the downstream communication bottleneck while maintaining upstream traffic comparable to the most efficient baselines.
Notably, ECPFL reduces the synchronous downstream volume from 87.7~MB to 1.5~MB on MNIST, from 79.7~MB to 1.2~MB on Fashion-MNIST, from 534.0~MB to 3.8~MB on CIFAR-10, and from 650.7~MB to 6.0~MB on SVHN, thereby achieving 58, 66, 140, and 109 fold reductions relative to FedAvg+CKKS.
Furthermore, ECPFL consistently lowers downstream traffic by 32 to 140 times across all secure baselines because CipherFetch shifts the dominant server-to-client payload into asynchronous background prefetching, and consequently selected clients fetch only the residual ciphertext blocks identified by the threshold-based sparse update mask.
As a result, the number of aggregated ciphertext batches falls to merely 20, 10, 37, and 105 on the four datasets, which is substantially smaller than the full-model aggregation counts of competing schemes and effectively confines the client download to the residual volume.

\subsubsection{End-to-End Training Time}
Regarding end-to-end training latency, the UT, DT, and TT values in Table~\ref{tab:perf_evaluation} are all 50-round totals measured at the slowest client, where UT and DT denote upload and download wall-clock time and TT denotes the sum of per-round slowest-client wall-clock time and therefore encompasses local training, encryption, aggregation, and communication.
ECPFL remarkably completes the 50 rounds on SVHN in 577.6~seconds, whereas FedAvg+CKKS, FedPHE, ESAFL, and MASER require 3141.6, 2445.1, 2363.5, and 3308.4~seconds, thus delivering speedups of 5.4, 5.0, 4.8, and 5.7 times respectively.
Although ECPFL is slower than the single-key baselines on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 because sticky client selection and threshold-based batch voting introduce additional client-side overhead, its synchronous downstream waiting time remains far below all competitors on every dataset.
Therefore, CipherFetch successfully removes the downstream bottleneck in communication-dominated scenarios while keeping overall latency within the same order of magnitude on smaller models.
