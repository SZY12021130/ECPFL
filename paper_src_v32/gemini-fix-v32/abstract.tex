\begin{abstract}
Cross-device federated learning (FL) suffers from substantial communication overhead between a central server and heterogeneous, bandwidth-constrained edge devices. Although upstream sparsification and downstream prefetching can alleviate this burden, integrating them into multi-key homomorphically encrypted FL introduces three challenges: heterogeneous sparse selections disrupt the spatial alignment required for SIMD aggregation, client subsampling and upload-phase dropouts leave missing key shares that break direct decryption under a shared aggregate key, and fixed-size packed ciphertexts prevent plaintext sparsity from directly reducing upstream and downstream prefetching traffic. To address these challenges, we propose ECPFL, a dropout-resilient secure FL framework that jointly optimizes upstream and downstream communication while preserving non-interactive local decryption under intermittent participation. On the upstream side, Efficient Sparse Packing (ESP) employs a Shamir zero-sharing-based secure voting mechanism to align selected update packs across clients and a Shamir secret sharing-based dropout compensation mechanism to reconstruct the exact aggregate encryption mask without peer-to-peer communication or decryption-share uploads. On the downstream side, Efficient Ciphertext Prefetching (ECP) enables intermittently available clients to asynchronously prefetch and homomorphically accumulate sparse encrypted model updates across rounds. Upon resuming training, clients recover the accumulated updates through one local decryption per pack without exposing plaintexts to the server. Experiments on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and SVHN demonstrate that ECPFL maintains competitive accuracy while reducing online communication volume by up to $10.7\times$ and accelerating end-to-end training by up to $5.7\times$ over the evaluated baselines.
\end{abstract}
